A working glossary · the recurring vocabulary

Glossary

The trilogy uses a specific vocabulary that runs across all four books, the explainer pages, and the synthesis essay. This page collects the recurring terms in one place, each with a short definition and a link onward to wherever the term lives in the rest of the site. Not a dictionary — a working reference. Terms are grouped thematically; an alphabetical index sits at the foot of the page.

If you encountered a term in a novel or an explainer and ended up here, the bold word inside the definition is usually the one to click first.

The core framework

The Field. The trilogy's name for the foundational ontological layer — that which is fundamental, of which matter, space, time, and individual minds are localized modes. Capitalized when the framework-specific sense is meant; lower-case "field" elsewhere. The Field is to consciousness what the electromagnetic field is to photons. See Synthesis §9.

Receiver model. The proposal that the brain does not produce consciousness but receives it — that conscious experience is the result of biological tissue coupling to a more fundamental field, with the body acting as a tuned antenna rather than a generator. The trilogy's central claim. Contrasted with the production model or productionism. See Synthesis, terminal lucidity, Levin.

Production model / productionism. The orthodox materialist position that consciousness is produced by neural activity — that the brain generates mind the way the liver generates bile. The position the receiver model responds to. See Crick & Koch's NCC program.

Antenna. The biological body considered as a tuned receiver for the Field. The metaphor is not loose: the body's architecture is built on phi-spiraled geometry (cochlea, DNA grooves, microtubules, fascia, phyllotaxis) which is exactly the geometry one would expect of a receiver whose job is to sample a continuous field at maximum coverage. See the golden ratio explainer.

Phi-tuned. Describing a biological structure (or, by extension, a person, a moment, a chord) whose proportions are organized around the golden ratio (φ ≈ 1.618). The trilogy's recurring image of the body as a φ-tuned antenna receiving the Field. See golden-ratio explainer.

φ-C (phi-tuned C). The specific frequency the trilogy circles around: 266.67 Hz, the C derived from φ-ratios rather than from standard equal-temperament A=440 tuning (which gives 261.63 Hz). The trilogy's claim is that the φ-C frequency is the resonant frequency the body is built to receive; Catherino's 72-tone work and Buckley's Hallelujah takes are the listening rooms in which the claim becomes audible. See the φ-tuned C explainer for the math, and Watch & Listen.

The cosmological architecture

Layer 0. The deepest layer in Limen's nested-reality cosmology — the unrendered, pre-spacetime substrate of which everything we experience is a rendering. The closest theological cognate is Eckhart's Gottheit, the Godhead-without-relation; the closest physics cognate is the pre-spatial structure that Arkani-Hamed's amplituhedron implies. See the amplituhedron explainer.

Rendering. The trilogy's word for what happens when Layer 0 produces local, spatially-extended, observer-facing reality. Spacetime is a render. So are objects, and possibly individual minds. The metaphor borrows from computer graphics deliberately. See Planck scale, Bekenstein bound, amplituhedron.

Render economy. The trilogy's framing of why the universe behaves the way it does at the quantum level: because rendering everything in continuous detail would be computationally prohibitive, reality is rendered only when observed, only to the resolution required, with the unrendered parts held in superposition. The compression principle behind quantum measurement, Planck-scale quantization, and the Bekenstein bound. See Synthesis §6 & §8.

Field cosmology. The umbrella term for any cosmology in which consciousness or information is the foundational layer and matter is derivative. Compatible programs include D'Ariano & Faggin's information-theoretic framework, Maria Strømme's Φ-field formalization, and Donald Hoffman's interface theory. See Synthesis §9.

The chord

The chord. The trilogy's recurring image for what happens when the Field is received cleanly through a tuned biological substrate. Literal in Numen, where a specific augmented architecture of three frequencies is held in coherence by attention; metaphorical elsewhere. The chord that refuses to resolve is the formal musical structure of an entanglement that the field is still rendering. See Watch & Listen (Scriabin, Ligeti, Buckley).

The augmented architecture / augmented chord. A chord built of three notes a major third apart (e.g., C–E–G♯). It has no single root and refuses to resolve to a tonic. The trilogy uses it as the formal musical analogue of an entangled system that cannot be locally decomposed. See Lennon's (Just Like) Starting Over, Scriabin's Prometheus, the chord scenes in Numen.

Cymatic. Pertaining to cymatics — the study of how sustained tones at specific frequencies organize granular material on a vibrating plate into stable geometric patterns. The visible analogue of frequency-as-form. See Chladni / cymatics explainer.

Time, retrocausality, and the 300-ms gap

The 300-ms gap. The interval Libet measured between the onset of the readiness potential and the conscious decision to move — about 350 milliseconds in the original experiment. Used for decades as evidence against free will. The trilogy reframes it as the latency of the Field rendering local choice through biological tissue: not the absence of agency but the time it takes the receiver to bring the rendered choice into local representation. See Libet explainer, Schurger reinterpretation.

The pre-event window. The trilogy's symmetric counterpart to the 300-ms gap: a roughly 300-ms window before a sensory or environmental event in which a sufficiently sensitive receiver (Lucía Reyes in Anima; the cymatic patterns in Numen) registers the event coming. Empirically scaffolded by the two-state vector formalism and the delayed-choice experiments. See TSVF, Wheeler delayed-choice, Manning 2015.

Retrocausality. The technical claim, made rigorous by Aharonov & Vaidman's two-state vector formalism and demonstrated experimentally by Wheeler's delayed-choice, that the present is constrained by both the past and the future — not in a way that lets us send signals backward in time, but in a way that means "what is happening now" includes information from the measurement that has not yet occurred. See TSVF explainer.

The body

Biocybernetic. Describing systems that combine biological substrate with cybernetic information processing. The architecture of the Numen character Sable; also the architecture, more broadly, of any organism considered as a closed-loop information system rather than a chemical machine. The Cortical Labs CL1 is biocybernetic in the literal industrial sense. See biocomputing & the bio-cybernetic interface.

EZ water (exclusion-zone water). Gerald Pollack's proposed "fourth phase" of water — a charge-separated, semi-liquid-crystalline layer that forms at hydrophilic surfaces and extends hundreds of microns. If real at biological scales, it provides a candidate medium for the cellular-memory and microtubule-coherence claims the receiver model needs. See EZ water consolidated explainer.

Bioelectric blueprint. Michael Levin's term for the field of cell-membrane potentials that patterns biological form alongside (and sometimes ahead of) DNA. The trilogy treats the bioelectric blueprint as the cellular-scale instantiation of the receiver model. See Levin explainer.

Orch-OR. Penrose-Hameroff's Orchestrated Objective Reduction: the hypothesis that consciousness arises from quantum-coherent processes in microtubules. Long dismissed as biologically implausible; revived by Bandyopadhyay & Hameroff's 2022 demonstration of microtubule vibrational coherence at biologically relevant temperatures. See Synthesis §9, EZ-water.

Phenomenology and the contemplative

The gift structure. The phenomenological signature Marín García identifies in Teresa of Ávila's mystical writings: experience that arrives without being produced — characterized by surprise, novelty, suddenness, absoluteness, inter-personal origin, and the failure of language. The trilogy treats this structure as the first-person fingerprint of receiver-mode consciousness. See Teresa explainer.

Infused vs. acquired (after Teresa). Teresa's distinction between acquired prayer (the intellect producing its own attention) and infused prayer (something given to the intellect from elsewhere). The grammatical inversion is the point: in the second mode the soul is the recipient, not the source. See Teresa explainer.

The noise. Teresa's word for what the productive intellect does to a receiver that is trying to listen: "running about with the intellect looking for many words and reflections." The trilogy treats this as the contemplative-tradition name for what the Libet experiment measures as the readiness potential. See Teresa explainer.

Sākshī. Sanskrit for witness. The Advaita Vedānta term for the unchanging awareness that observes thought and sensation without being identified with them. Kiran Sākshī in Fragile Light is named for the concept. The contemplative cognate of the receiver model.

Anomalous evidence

Terminal lucidity. The clinical phenomenon in which patients with severe, longstanding cortical compromise (advanced dementia, late-stage psychiatric illness) unexpectedly regain coherent speech, orientation, and recognition in the hours or days before death. Documented across the medical literature for over a century, systematized by Nahm 2012, expanded to hundreds of cases by Batthyány 2023. See explainer.

Acquired savant syndrome. The emergence of savant-level capacities (mathematical, musical, artistic) in previously neurotypical individuals after brain injury, stroke, or frontotemporal dementia. The "capacity appearing after damage" phenomenon the production model has no place to put. See Treffert explainer.

The 40-bit paradox. Zimmermann's textbook estimate that the human nervous system processes roughly 10⁷ bits per second of sensory input while conscious awareness handles approximately 50. The five-to-six orders of magnitude of compression between input and experience that any consciousness theory has to explain. Sometimes called the user-illusion after Nørretranders' book.

Music and frequency

Spectral music. The compositional school founded by Grisey, Murail, and their colleagues at IRCAM in the 1970s. Treats the overtone series of a single sound as the primary compositional material, building works from spectral analysis rather than from melodic or harmonic abstraction. The musical translation of frequency is structure, not coordinate. See Watch & Listen.

Micropolyphony. Ligeti's term for the cluster-texture technique in Atmosphères and Lux Aeterna (used by Kubrick in 2001): dozens of independent slow voices layered so densely that no melody or harmony emerges, only a continuous field of sound with internal motion. See Watch & Listen.

Just intonation. Any tuning system in which intervals are tuned to the exact whole-number ratios of the harmonic series (2:1 octave, 3:2 fifth, 5:4 major third, etc.) rather than the equal-tempered approximations used in standard Western music. La Monte Young, Catherine Lamb, and the trilogy's φ-C frequencies all live in this territory.

Polychromatic music. Dolores Catherino's term for music with more than the 12 pitches-per-octave that equal temperament permits — her own work uses 31, 72, and 106 pitches per octave. The trilogy's closest contemporary listening room.
