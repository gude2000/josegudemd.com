A working glossary · the recurring vocabulary

Glossary

The trilogy uses a specific vocabulary that runs across all four books, the explainer pages, and the synthesis essay. This page collects the recurring terms in one place, each with a short definition and a link onward to wherever the term lives in the rest of the site. Not a dictionary — a working reference. Terms are grouped thematically; an alphabetical index sits at the foot of the page.

If you encountered a term in a novel or an explainer and ended up here, the bold word inside the definition is usually the one to click first.

Many of these terms belong to the novels: they name the premise the fiction works inside, not established findings. Where a definition makes a claim about the world, it now carries the same status labels the Synthesis uses — Established Hypothesis Open Speculative Novels' premise. Terms from the author's nonfiction are collected in their own section, just before the index.

The core framework

The Field. Novels' premise The trilogy's name for the foundational ontological layer — that which is fundamental, of which matter, space, time, and individual minds are localized modes. Capitalized when the framework-specific sense is meant; lower-case "field" elsewhere. In the novels, the Field is to consciousness what the electromagnetic field is to photons. See Synthesis §9.

Receiver model. The proposal that the brain does not produce consciousness but receives it — that conscious experience is the result of biological tissue coupling to a more fundamental field, with the body acting as a tuned antenna rather than a generator. The novels' central premise; as a scientific claim it is Speculative, since no current evidence decides between it and production views. Contrasted with the production model or productionism. See Synthesis, terminal lucidity, Levin.

Production model / productionism. The orthodox materialist position that consciousness is produced by neural activity — that the brain generates mind the way the liver generates bile. The position the receiver model responds to. See Crick & Koch's NCC program.

Antenna. Novels' premise The biological body considered as a tuned receiver for the Field. It is the novels' governing image. The geometries the novels point to (phyllotaxis, spirals, the golden ratio) are real in some cases and overstated in others, and none of them is evidence that the body receives a field; see the golden ratio explainer and Synthesis §10.

Phi-tuned. Describing a biological structure (or, by extension, a person, a moment, a chord) whose proportions are organized around the golden ratio (φ ≈ 1.618). The trilogy's recurring image of the body as a φ-tuned antenna receiving the Field (novels' premise). See golden-ratio explainer.

φ-C (phi-tuned C). The specific frequency the trilogy circles around: 266.67 Hz, the C derived from φ-ratios rather than from standard equal-temperament A=440 tuning (which gives 261.63 Hz). In the novels, the φ-C frequency is the resonant frequency the body is built to receive Novels' premise; there is no evidence that the body is tuned to any particular frequency. Catherino's 72-tone work and Buckley's Hallelujah takes are the listening rooms in which the claim becomes audible. See the φ-tuned C explainer for the math, and Watch & Listen.

The cosmological architecture

Layer 0. Novels' premise The deepest layer in Limen's nested-reality cosmology — the unrendered, pre-spacetime substrate of which everything we experience is a rendering. The closest theological cognate is Eckhart's Gottheit, the Godhead-without-relation; the novels draw an analogy with the amplituhedron, a geometric structure in a particular theoretical model from which locality emerges. See the amplituhedron explainer.

Rendering. Novels' premise The trilogy's word for what happens when Layer 0 produces local, spatially-extended, observer-facing reality. Spacetime is a render. So are objects, and possibly individual minds. The metaphor borrows from computer graphics deliberately. See Planck scale, Bekenstein bound, amplituhedron.

Render economy. Novels' premise The trilogy's framing of why the universe behaves the way it does at the quantum level: because rendering everything in continuous detail would be computationally prohibitive, reality is rendered only when observed, only to the resolution required, with the unrendered parts held in superposition. In the novels, the compression principle behind quantum measurement, Planck-scale quantization, and the Bekenstein bound; in physics, each of these has explanations with no rendering in them. See Synthesis §6 & §8.

Field cosmology. The umbrella term for any cosmology in which consciousness or information is the foundational layer and matter is derivative. Compatible programs include D'Ariano & Faggin's information-theoretic framework, Donald Hoffman's interface theory, and Maria Strømme's Φ-field formalisation (retracted by its journal in 2026). All are Speculative. See Synthesis §9.

The chord

The chord. The trilogy's recurring image for what happens when the Field is received cleanly through a tuned biological substrate. Literal in Numen, where a specific augmented architecture of three frequencies is held in coherence by attention; metaphorical elsewhere. In the novels, the chord that refuses to resolve is the musical image of an entanglement the field is still rendering. See Watch & Listen (Scriabin, Ligeti, Buckley).

The augmented architecture / augmented chord. A chord built of three notes a major third apart (e.g., C–E–G♯). It has no single root and refuses to resolve to a tonic. The trilogy uses it as the formal musical analogue of an entangled system that cannot be locally decomposed. See Lennon's (Just Like) Starting Over, Scriabin's Prometheus, the chord scenes in Numen.

Cymatic. Pertaining to cymatics — the study of how sustained tones at specific frequencies organize granular material on a vibrating plate into stable geometric patterns. The visible analogue of frequency-as-form. See Chladni / cymatics explainer.

Time, retrocausality, and the 300-ms gap

The 300-ms gap. The interval Libet measured between the onset of the readiness potential and the conscious decision to move — about 350 milliseconds in the original experiment. Used for decades as evidence against free will. Schurger's accumulator model suggests the readiness potential may not mark a decision at all. The novels reframe the gap as the latency of the Field rendering local choice through biological tissue Novels' premise: not the absence of agency but the time it takes the receiver to bring the rendered choice into local representation. See Libet explainer, Schurger reinterpretation.

The pre-event window. The trilogy's symmetric counterpart to the 300-ms gap: a roughly 300-ms window before a sensory or environmental event in which a sufficiently sensitive receiver (Lucía Reyes in Anima; the cymatic patterns in Numen) registers the event coming. Novels' premise Inspired by the two-state vector formalism and the delayed-choice experiments, which do not themselves show any such window. See TSVF, Wheeler delayed-choice, Manning 2015.

Retrocausality. Speculative The proposal that the present is constrained by the future as well as the past. Aharonov & Vaidman's two-state vector formalism is a time-symmetric reformulation of quantum mechanics with the same predictions as the standard theory Established; the delayed-choice experiments confirm quantum mechanics exactly. Neither forces a retrocausal reading: standard quantum mechanics accounts for the results with no influence running backward, and whether the backward-evolving state is physically real is Open. See TSVF explainer, delayed choice.

The body

Biocybernetic. Describing systems that combine biological substrate with cybernetic information processing. The architecture of the Numen character Sable; also the architecture, more broadly, of any organism considered as a closed-loop information system rather than a chemical machine. The Cortical Labs CL1 is biocybernetic in the literal industrial sense. See biocomputing & the bio-cybernetic interface.

EZ water (exclusion-zone water). Gerald Pollack's proposed "fourth phase" of water — a charge-separated, semi-liquid-crystalline layer that forms at hydrophilic surfaces and extends hundreds of microns. If real at biological scales, it provides a candidate medium for the cellular-memory and microtubule-coherence claims the receiver model needs. See EZ water consolidated explainer.

Bioelectric blueprint. Michael Levin's term for the field of cell-membrane potentials that patterns biological form alongside (and sometimes ahead of) DNA. The trilogy treats the bioelectric blueprint as the cellular-scale instantiation of the receiver model. See Levin explainer.

Orch-OR. Penrose-Hameroff's Orchestrated Objective Reduction: the hypothesis that consciousness arises from quantum-coherent processes in microtubules. Hypothesis Testable and contested: laboratory work has reported microtubule resonances and collective optical effects, and a 2024 animal study linked microtubule stabilization to delayed anaesthesia, but functional quantum coherence in the living brain has not been shown, and a 2021 underground experiment ruled out the simplest version of the collapse model it relies on. See Synthesis §9, EZ-water.

Phenomenology and the contemplative

The gift structure. The phenomenological signature Marín García identifies in Teresa of Ávila's mystical writings: experience that arrives without being produced — characterized by surprise, novelty, suddenness, absoluteness, inter-personal origin, and the failure of language. The novels treat this structure as the first-person fingerprint of receiver-mode consciousness Novels' premise. See Teresa explainer.

Infused vs. acquired (after Teresa). Teresa's distinction between acquired prayer (the intellect producing its own attention) and infused prayer (something given to the intellect from elsewhere). The grammatical inversion is the point: in the second mode the soul is the recipient, not the source. See Teresa explainer.

The noise. Teresa's word for what the productive intellect does to a receiver that is trying to listen: "running about with the intellect looking for many words and reflections." The novels read this as the contemplative-tradition name for what the Libet experiment measures as the readiness potential; that is an analogy, not a finding. See Teresa explainer.

Sākshī. Sanskrit for witness. The Advaita Vedānta term for the unchanging awareness that observes thought and sensation without being identified with them. Kiran Sākshī in Fragile Light is named for the concept. The contemplative cognate of the receiver model.

Anomalous evidence

Terminal lucidity. The clinical phenomenon in which patients with severe, longstanding cortical compromise (advanced dementia, late-stage psychiatric illness) unexpectedly regain coherent speech, orientation, and recognition in the hours or days before death. Reported across the medical literature for over a century, collected by Nahm and colleagues (2012) and Batthyány (2023). Open The reports are numerous; most are retrospective, the frequency is unknown, and no mechanism has been tested. See explainer.

Acquired savant syndrome. The emergence of savant-level capacities (mathematical, musical, artistic) in previously neurotypical individuals after brain injury, stroke, or frontotemporal dementia. Rare and real Established; most researchers explain it as release from inhibition or network reorganization, which remain hypotheses. See Treffert explainer.

The 40-bit paradox. Zimmermann's textbook estimate that the human nervous system processes roughly 10⁷ bits per second of sensory input while conscious awareness handles approximately 50. The five-to-six orders of magnitude of compression between input and experience that any consciousness theory has to explain. Sometimes called the user-illusion after Nørretranders' book.

Music and frequency

Spectral music. The compositional school founded by Grisey, Murail, and their colleagues at IRCAM in the 1970s. Treats the overtone series of a single sound as the primary compositional material, building works from spectral analysis rather than from melodic or harmonic abstraction. The musical translation of frequency is structure, not coordinate. See Watch & Listen.

Micropolyphony. Ligeti's term for the cluster-texture technique in Atmosphères and Lux Aeterna (used by Kubrick in 2001): dozens of independent slow voices layered so densely that no melody or harmony emerges, only a continuous field of sound with internal motion. See Watch & Listen.

Just intonation. Any tuning system in which intervals are tuned to the exact whole-number ratios of the harmonic series (2:1 octave, 3:2 fifth, 5:4 major third, etc.) rather than the equal-tempered approximations used in standard Western music. La Monte Young, Catherine Lamb, and the trilogy's φ-C frequencies all live in this territory.

Polychromatic music. Dolores Catherino's term for music with more than the 12 pitches-per-octave that equal temperament permits — her own work uses 31, 72, and 106 pitches per octave. The trilogy's closest contemporary listening room.

Quantum interpretation & physics anomalies

Wave function (ψ). The complex-valued mathematical object that encodes the quantum state of a system. For one particle, a function over three-dimensional space; for N particles, a function over 3N-dimensional configuration space. |ψ|² gives the probability density of measurement outcomes. The wave function does not "wave on" anything in the classical sense, and what it represents is the central interpretive question of quantum mechanics. See What Does the Wave Wave On?

Configuration space. The high-dimensional mathematical space the wave function actually lives on. For a system of N particles, configuration space has 3N dimensions. The interference pattern of the double-slit experiment appears on a 2D screen in 3D space, but the wave producing it lives in this much higher-dimensional space — which is the technical reason "what does it wave on" has no easy answer.

Pilot wave. The de Broglie (1927) — Bohm (1952) interpretation of quantum mechanics. Particles have definite positions at all times and are guided through space by a real physical wave that carries information about the entire experimental setup. Mathematically equivalent to standard quantum mechanics; ontologically much more transparent. The Couder–Fort oil-droplet experiments at Paris Diderot (2006) gave a classical fluid analog. See What Does the Wave Wave On? and Glitches in Reality.

Implicate order. David Bohm's term for the pre-spatial, pre-temporal substrate from which three-dimensional reality and ordinary matter unfold. Particles and waves alike are surface expressions ("explicate") of an underlying enfolded structure ("implicate"). The pilot wave is the visible signature of the implicate order. The closest physics-language cognate of what the trilogy calls the Field. Bohm, Wholeness and the Implicate Order (1980).

Many-Worlds. Hugh Everett's 1957 interpretation: the wave function never collapses; every quantum event branches the universe into all possible outcomes, each realized in its own parallel world. Parsimonious in postulates (no collapse axiom), extravagant in entities (uncountable branches). Raises a consciousness question — whether all parallel versions of you are equally conscious — that most defenders have not solved.

Objective collapse. The family of interpretations (Ghirardi-Rimini-Weber, Penrose's gravitational collapse, Diósi) in which the wave function is physically real and collapses spontaneously at some threshold (mass, gravitational self-energy, environmental complexity). The most experimentally testable alternative to Copenhagen; an underground experiment at Gran Sasso (Donadi and colleagues, 2021) ruled out the simplest, parameter-free version of the Diósi–Penrose model, while versions with added parameters survive.

Holographic principle. Hypothesis 't Hooft and Susskind's proposal that the physics inside a region can be fully described by information on its boundary, generalizing the area-scaling of black-hole entropy. Maldacena's AdS/CFT correspondence (1997) realizes it exactly in anti-de Sitter space, which is not our universe; whether it holds here is open. Mainstream and heavily cited theoretical physics. The novels read it as the fingerprint of a universe with finite storage Novels' premise. See Simulation Hypothesis #10.

Bekenstein bound. Established (as theory) Jacob Bekenstein's 1981 limit on the entropy, and so the information, that a region of given size and energy can contain. The related area law for black-hole entropy (Bekenstein and Hawking, 1970s) later motivated the holographic bound. Supported by black-hole thermodynamics; not experimentally tested. See Bekenstein explainer, Simulation Hypothesis #10.

It from bit. John Archibald Wheeler's 1989 formulation: physical reality (it) emerges from binary information (bit). Every particle, every field, every spacetime structure derives, at bottom, from yes/no answers to yes/no questions. A philosophical programme, Speculative, not a result. It inspired work on holography and is often cited in simulation arguments, but information being fundamental would not by itself make the universe a computation.

Mathematical universe hypothesis. Speculative Max Tegmark's claim, published in Foundations of Physics (2008), that the universe is not described by mathematics — the universe is mathematics. There is no additional physical substrate beneath the equations; the equations are the reality. Wigner's "unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics" (1960) becomes obvious if the structures we use to describe reality just are reality. See Simulation Hypothesis #3.

Bostrom simulation argument. Nick Bostrom (2003): a formal trilemma. Either (1) civilizations almost always go extinct before reaching simulation capability, or (2) those that reach it almost always choose not to run ancestor simulations, or (3) we are almost certainly already inside one. Speculative The argument is a philosophical one with a probabilistic structure: valid given its premises, and only as strong as they are — in particular the premise that simulated minds would be conscious. See Simulation Hypothesis #4.

Wheeler's participatory universe. John Archibald Wheeler's mature position: no phenomenon is a real phenomenon until it is an observed phenomenon. Speculative On this view the universe requires observers to bring itself into definite existence; Wheeler's "observer" was a registering device, not necessarily a conscious mind. His delayed-choice thought experiment (1978) has been realized in Paris (2007) and at the Australian National University (2015); the results confirm quantum mechanics, and do not single out Wheeler's participatory reading. See delayed-choice explainer, Simulation Hypothesis #8.

Anthropic principle. The observation that the universe must have properties compatible with the existence of observers because we observe it. The weak form is a tautology used to explain why we find ourselves in a life-permitting universe; the strong form claims the universe is required to develop observers; the participatory form (Wheeler) makes the observers part of the universe's mechanism of self-actualization. Combined with fine-tuning, some read it as implying the constants were set to produce conscious observers; others read the same facts as selection among many universes, or as chance. Open See Simulation Hypothesis #1.

Fine-structure constant (α). A dimensionless number, approximately 1/137, that governs the strength of the electromagnetic interaction between light and matter. Not derived from any deeper theory — measured, not predicted. Richard Feynman called it "a magic number that comes to us with no understanding by man." On some calculations, a change of a few per cent would disrupt the stellar processes that make carbon. One of the twenty-six fundamental constants the universe sits on without explanation. See Simulation Hypothesis #2.

Penrose number. Roger Penrose's calculated probability of the universe's initial low-entropy state at the Big Bang occurring by random chance: 1 in 1010123. Written out, the number would have about 10123 digits — far more digits than there are atoms (about 1080) in the observable universe. Not a large number — the word large does not apply. A probability functionally indistinguishable from impossible, on Penrose's estimate; what it implies is Open.

Error-correcting codes (in physics). Established (as mathematics) Around 2010, S. James Gates Jr. and collaborators showed that certain graphical representations of supersymmetry (adinkras) are related to doubly-even self-dual binary error-correcting codes, a family of codes used in digital communication. It is a genuine mathematical observation about supersymmetry, which has not been confirmed by experiment. Reading it as a sign that we live in a simulation is Speculative, a possibility Gates has discussed rather than a finding. See Simulation Hypothesis #9.

Path integral / principle of least action. Richard Feynman (1942): a particle moving from A to B does not take one path; it simultaneously explores every possible path through spacetime, weighted toward the path of least action. The mathematical foundation of quantum field theory and the most precisely tested theory in science. By analogy, some writers describe this as optimization, as if the universe minimized computational load; that is a metaphor Speculative, not part of the physics. See Simulation Hypothesis #7.

Mandela effect. The phenomenon by which large groups of people share identical false memories of cultural details — most famously, the false memory that Nelson Mandela died in prison in the 1980s. Conventional explanation: constructive memory and social reinforcement converge on shared errors. The trilogy treats the Mandela effect not as evidence the past was edited but as a clinical reminder that memory is reconstructive in ways that pressure our naive realism. See Glitches #3.

Bullet Cluster. The 2006 observation by Clowe and colleagues of two galaxy clusters colliding, in which the gravitational mass (mapped by gravitational lensing) is spatially separated from the visible matter (mapped by X-ray). The first direct visual evidence of dark matter — and the cleanest demonstration that whatever the universe is made of, it is not just the matter we can see. See Glitches #5.

CMB anomalies. Established / Open Statistical features in the cosmic microwave background that the simplest cosmological model does not predict: an unusually cold region in Eridanus (the cold spot, roughly a 1-in-100 to three-sigma anomaly depending on the analysis), a hemispherical power asymmetry, and a low quadrupole aligned with the octopole and with the plane of the solar system (the so-called axis of evil). They persist across two independent space missions (WMAP, Planck); whether they reflect new physics or the many patterns one could have looked for is open. See Glitches #8.

Black hole information paradox. Hawking's 1974 finding that black holes emit thermal radiation and slowly evaporate, combined with quantum mechanics' demand that information must be conserved, produces a contradiction: when a black hole evaporates entirely into thermal radiation, the information of what fell in has apparently been destroyed. Recent work on the holographic principle and the 2019 "island formula" suggests resolution paths; none is yet uncontested. See Glitches #9.

The Field. Novels' premise See also the entry at the top of this glossary. In the context of the new companion essays, the Field sits at the convergence of Bohm's implicate order, Tegmark's mathematical universe, and the receiver model: the substrate is informational, mathematical, possibly experiential at its base; particles and waves are patterns in it; the trilogy's framework treats this substrate as the same one different disciplines have been describing in their own vocabularies for centuries.

Vertical samsara. Novels' premise Jose Gude's own term — a refinement of the classical contemplative concept of samsara (the recurrence of pattern through successive lives). Where the received tradition leaves the trajectory implicit, vertical samsara names the wrinkle: the wave pattern does not merely recur — it reincarnates into progressively more evolved instances of itself, each iteration carrying forward what the previous one learned and shedding what it no longer needs. Information is not lost across lives; it is progressively edited toward a more complete version of itself. Each return is a draft. The framework parallels the holographic resolutions of the black-hole information paradox in physics: consciousness is conserved across a surface the standard instruments cannot read, with the edits providing direction to the encoding. The fictional case is Anima's Lucía Reyes — the seven-year-old born with a birthmark matching her father's fatal wound, carrying verifiable knowledge of his experiences. The novels' point of departure is the archive assembled by Ian Stevenson MD at the University of Virginia (continued today by Jim Tucker MD): roughly 2,500 cases of children describing previous lives, whose interpretation is contested (see Synthesis §3). In Biological Individuation Theory (§15.9) the author revisits the idea and argues that recurrence would be someone again, not this one again: it would not carry the person. See Glitches #9 — the black hole information paradox.

Quantized signature. Novels' premise The trilogy's term for the discrete, address-string-like identity through which a receiver couples to the Field. Even where the underlying pitch space, configuration space, or genetic space is an analog continuum, every physical receiver resolves into discrete permitted states. The principle generalizes across scales: the audible case is the augmented chord — E / G♯ / C at φ-ratios, three permitted frequencies out of the infinite pitch continuum; the biological case is DNA — a quaternary code (four symbols, A · T · C · G, carrying two bits per base), in which, in the novels, each genome is a unique signature through which the Field can individually couple Novels' premise; the physical case is quantum spin and charge — strictly quantized at every level we can probe. Same architecture, three vocabularies, three reasons that continuous reality keeps resolving into discrete addressable states. See Simulation Hypothesis #6.

From the author's nonfiction

Terms from Biological Individuation Theory (BIT), the author's theory book, still in revision. Definitions follow the book's own glossary.

Status labels. The vocabulary BIT uses to mark every claim, and that this site now uses too: Established supported by the evidence cited; Hypothesis a testable proposal; Open unresolved; Speculative an interpretation the evidence does not test. (BIT also marks ethical proposals as normative rather than empirical claims.) This site adds Novels' premise for what the fiction assumes.

Phenomenal individuation. Experience being bounded and organized as one subject's, from one locus, belonging to one trajectory: someone here, now. Distinct from biological individuation, a living system becoming and remaining one self-maintaining unit with a boundary, viability and a continuing trajectory.

Conservative BIT. Hypothesis The claim that the boundary, unity and continuity of an individual conscious perspective depend on the coordinated, self-maintaining organization of the living organism, and are not fully determined by neural information processing considered apart from that organization. Tested first by prediction on held-out data, then by intervention.

Strong BIT. Speculative Conservative BIT plus one further claim: that phenomenality is not generated by the organism, and that biological organization individuates it into bounded centres of experience.

The firewall. BIT's rule that evidence the organism individuates experience is not evidence that experience exists apart from the organism. Even complete success for Conservative BIT would leave Strong BIT where it stands.

Biological aperture. The recursively coordinated organization of biological constraints through which a living system maintains a bounded perspective, a world of relevance and a continuing trajectory. BIT's alternative to the receiver: it does not merely narrow something broad, it makes it particular.

Decombination problem. How one phenomenal ground could become many bounded subjects. The central problem any consciousness-first view, Strong BIT included, inherits.

Open-Bookends Principle. Whether a phenomenal ground precedes and outlasts biological apertures is open. Whether a person does is not open in the same way: on BIT's account the person begins with biological individuation and ends with it, unless an independent carrier of personal continuity is demonstrated.

Trajectory sovereignty. An ethical proposal: an individuated being should have meaningful participation in determining its own continuing trajectory, and no other agent may appropriate it.
