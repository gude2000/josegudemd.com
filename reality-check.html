Reading · reality check · four tiers

Reality Check

The novels mix established science, working hypotheses, speculation and imagined futures in a single story. That is what fiction is allowed to do — but the reader is entitled to know which is which. What follows is a calibration tool in four tiers, using the same labels as the author's Biological Individuation Theory: Established, Hypothesis and Open, Speculative, and what the novels imagine.

Revised October 2026. An earlier version placed several contested results in the established tier and argued that the established evidence alone made a materialist account of consciousness untenable. That was not accurate, and this version corrects it. The audio narration below was recorded for the earlier version.

Tier 1 · Established

What the evidence supports

Peer-reviewed, replicated, or standard physics and clinical observation. The novels use these as ground. Being established does not make any of them evidence for the novels' premise; each is labelled for what it shows, and where its interpretation is speculative, that is marked separately.

Quantum entanglement

Established Two particles can share a quantum state so that measurements on them stay correlated across any distance, more strongly than any local theory allows — though no signal passes between them, and entanglement cannot be used to send one. Alain Aspect's 1981–82 Paris experiments were among the foundational tests, recognized with the 2022 Nobel Prize; China's Micius satellite distributed entangled photons between ground stations about 1,200 km apart in 2017. In the trilogy: Kiran Sākshī in Fragile Light mirrors Luz Paz across the distance between stars — entanglement written into character. → Glitches #2

Quantum tunneling

Established Particles cross energy barriers they classically could not, with a calculable probability. Tunnelling is part of how the sun fuses hydrogen, how alpha decay happens, and how some transistors and every scanning tunnelling microscope work. It is standard quantum mechanics, not an anomaly for physics. In the trilogy: referenced as the textbook case in Limen's opening physics chapter; the trilogy treats it as one of the standing scandals classical materialism cannot absorb. → Glitches in Reality — adjacent anomalies

The Bekenstein bound · the holographic principle

Established (as theory) Jacob Bekenstein's work in the 1970s, completed by Hawking, showed that a black hole's entropy scales with the area of its horizon, and Bekenstein proposed a general bound on the information a region can hold. Hypothesis The holographic principle ('t Hooft, Susskind) generalizes this; Juan Maldacena's 1997 AdS/CFT correspondence gave it an exact form in anti-de Sitter space, which is not our universe. Mainstream theoretical physics, with whether holography applies to our world still open. In the trilogy: Limen's twenty-seven two-dimensional diagrams are the holographic principle applied to consciousness — each diagram encodes a three-dimensional structure of the field on a two-dimensional surface. → Simulation #10

James Gates' error-correcting codes in supersymmetric equations

Established (as mathematics) S. James Gates Jr. and collaborators found that certain graphical representations of supersymmetry (“adinkras”) are related to doubly-even self-dual binary error-correcting codes, the same family of codes used in digital communication. It is a genuine mathematical observation about supersymmetry, a theory not yet confirmed by experiment. Speculative Reading it as a sign that the universe is engineered or simulated is an interpretation, which Gates himself has discussed as a possibility, not a finding. In the trilogy: referenced through Limen's convergence thesis — the universe's fundamental mathematics carries an engineering signature it has no obvious reason to carry. → Simulation #9

Cosmic microwave background anomalies

Established The CMB contains statistical features the simplest cosmological model does not predict — the Eridanus cold spot, the low quadrupole and its alignment with the octopole, a hemispherical power asymmetry — found by both WMAP and Planck. Open Their significance is debated, typically two to three standard deviations, and some may reflect the many patterns one could have looked for. In the trilogy: referenced in Limen's physics chapter as evidence the universe's first light contains patterns it should not contain. → Glitches #8

Hafele-Keating time dilation · GPS engineering

Established Caesium clocks flown around the world in 1971 returned showing the asymmetry relativity predicts. GPS satellite clocks run about 38 microseconds a day fast relative to the ground; uncorrected, positions would drift by roughly ten kilometres a day. In the trilogy: Numen's journal-across-eight-years operates on the same architecture — time held differently in two frames, the asymmetry visible only when they meet. → Glitches #4

The Schumann resonance

Established Winfried Otto Schumann predicted in 1952 that the cavity between the Earth's surface and the ionosphere supports electromagnetic resonances, with a fundamental near 7.83 Hz, driven by lightning. They were measured in the early 1960s and have been monitored since. Speculative Any link between them and the brain's similar-frequency rhythms is not established. In the trilogy: Limen's receiver-as-antenna argument sits on this — the brain's slow rhythms inhabit the same low-Hz band as the planet's cavity. → Schumann resonance explainer

Michael Levin's bioelectric program at Tufts

Established Levin's laboratory has shown in many peer-reviewed papers that patterns of voltage across cell membranes help control anatomy: altering them can change where eyes and other features form in frog embryos, and can produce planaria that keep regenerating two heads in later rounds of cutting. Trained planaria have been reported to retain some training after regenerating a new head. Hypothesis Levin's broader claim, that problem-solving competence exists at every biological scale, is a productive hypothesis. In the trilogy: the cellular-scale instantiation of the receiver model; Limen's biology section rests on Levin's work as the experimental floor under the field hypothesis. → Levin explainer

Cymatics

Established Ernst Chladni showed in 1787, and later work has refined, that vibrating plates organize sand into stable patterns set by the frequency. It is ordinary standing-wave physics, and beautiful. In the trilogy: Amara Osei's resonant-frequency work in Anima Chapter III; the cymatic patterns embedded in the Webb fractal triangle Alex inherits in Numen. → Cymatics explainer

Brain–computer interfaces in clinical use

Established Cortical Labs' CL1 (living neurons cultured on silicon), implanted brain–computer interfaces such as Neuralink's, and Medtronic's adaptive deep-brain stimulation are real systems in research or clinical use. What they are not doing — showing any sign of experience of their own — is the fictional part. In the trilogy: Numen's hybrid intelligences (Sable, Alma, the Mirror) extrapolate forward from these existing platforms. → Biocomputing & wetware explainer

Tier 2 · Hypothesis and open questions

The growing edge

Testable proposals not yet confirmed, and questions where the evidence is mixed or missing. Some of these will move into Tier 1; some will be revised or abandoned. The novels treat them as live possibilities, and the reader should too — no more, no less.

Quantum biology — photosynthesis coherence

Open In 2007 Graham Fleming's Berkeley group reported long-lived quantum coherence in a photosynthetic complex (measured at 77 K, far below body temperature), and the result was widely read as photosynthesis exploiting quantum effects. Later work substantially revised that reading: the long-lived signals were found to be mostly vibrational, with electronic coherence lasting only tens of femtoseconds (Duan and colleagues, 2017). Whether coherence matters functionally for energy transfer remains debated. In the trilogy: Limen's "Applied Science: The Body as Frequency System" chapter; Amara Osei's resonant-frequency work in Anima Chapter III is its experimental analog. → Reading

Quantum biology — the avian magnetic compass

Hypothesis The leading explanation of how migratory birds sense the Earth's magnetic field is a quantum radical-pair mechanism in cryptochrome proteins in the eye. The case has grown strong — cryptochrome 4 from a migratory songbird has been shown to be magnetically sensitive in the laboratory (Xu and colleagues, 2021) — but the mechanism has not been demonstrated in a living, navigating bird. In the trilogy: part of Limen's broader case that biology routinely runs the kind of quantum processes physics says biology cannot. → Reading

Microtubule vibrational coherence at body temperature

Hypothesis Anirban Bandyopadhyay's group has reported resonances in microtubules, and later work has measured collective optical effects (superradiance) in microtubule networks; a 2024 rat study found that a microtubule-stabilizing drug delayed anaesthetic-induced unconsciousness. These results are suggestive and debated, several are unreplicated, and none demonstrates functional quantum coherence in a living brain. In the trilogy: Senna Park introduces Orch-OR to José in Anima Chapter VIII; the chord-resonance experiments in Numen depend on it. → Bandyopadhyay explainer

Terminal lucidity

Open Reports span a century of medical literature, were collected by Michael Nahm and colleagues (2012), named paradoxical lucidity by a 2019 workgroup convened by the US National Institute on Aging, and gathered in book length in Alexander Batthyány's Threshold (2023). Patients with severe dementia or other long-standing impairment briefly regain coherent speech and recognition near death. The reports are numerous enough to take seriously; most are retrospective, the frequency is unknown, and no mechanism has been tested. In the trilogy: Mr. Martínez in Anima Chapter IV is the clinical case; the phenomenon underwrites the receiver-model argument from Anima's opening pages onward. → Terminal lucidity explainer

The Stevenson / Tucker University of Virginia past-life case database

Established (archive) / Speculative (interpretation) Ian Stevenson founded the programme at the University of Virginia, continued by Jim Tucker; it documents some 2,500 cases of young children describing the lives of deceased people, a subset with birthmarks said to match fatal wounds. The archive is large and carefully assembled. Whether the details reflect anything beyond normal transmission, cultural shaping and after-the-fact investigation is contested; Paul Edwards's critique and Stephen Braude's defence are the essential pair. In the trilogy: Lucía Reyes in Anima Chapter V is fiction's case — the seven-year-old born with a birthmark matching her dead father's fatal wound, carrying his memories. → Glitches #9 — vertical samsara

Penrose-Hameroff Orch-OR

Hypothesis Orchestrated Objective Reduction proposes that consciousness depends on quantum processes in microtubules, ended by a gravity-related collapse. Unusually among such proposals, it is testable: an underground experiment at Gran Sasso (Donadi and colleagues, 2021) ruled out the simplest, parameter-free version of the collapse model it relies on, while the microtubule results above keep the biological side alive. A live, contested hypothesis; most neuroscientists do not accept it. In the trilogy: Senna Park introduces it to José in Anima Chapter VIII; the theory underwrites the entire chord-substrate argument in Numen. → Synthesis §9

Pilot wave · Bohmian mechanics

Open De Broglie's 1927 proposal, completed by Bohm in 1952: particles have definite positions guided by a real wave. It makes the same predictions as standard quantum mechanics for non-relativistic systems and is a respected minority interpretation; its explicit non-locality and the difficulty of extending it to relativistic fields are among the reasons most physicists prefer others. Couder and Fort's oil-droplet experiments (2006) are a striking classical analogue, though they do not reproduce entanglement. In the trilogy: the fractal Webb triangle Alex inherits in Numen is the visible signature of a pilot-wave-like substrate — pattern from the implicate order made explicit. → What Does the Wave Wave On?

Fine-tuning of the 26 fundamental constants · Penrose's calculation

Open Many physicists accept that life-permitting universes occupy a narrow range of some constants, though how narrow is itself debated; Roger Penrose estimated the improbability of the universe's low-entropy initial state at 1 in 1010123. What fine-tuning means — chance, selection among many universes, or something else — is unresolved. In the trilogy: Limen's "The Architecture" treats the 26 numbers as the parameters typed into the simulation. → Simulation #2

Yamanaka-factor cellular reprogramming · partial age reversal

Hypothesis Shinya Yamanaka's 2006 four-factor reprogramming earned a Nobel Prize; partial reprogramming has reversed some markers of ageing in animal studies, and companies such as Altos Labs and Life Biosciences are working toward clinical use. No human trial has shown whole-body age reversal. In the trilogy: the rejuvenated rats Luz Paz finds in Fragile Light are the Tier 4 extrapolation of this Tier 2 trajectory. → Reading — aging research

Retrocausality · the two-state vector formalism

Open Aharonov and Vaidman's two-state vector formalism describes a quantum system using states evolving both forward and backward in time, and predicts the same results as standard quantum mechanics. Wheeler's delayed-choice experiment has been realized with photons (Paris, 2007) and single atoms (Australian National University, 2015); the results match quantum mechanics exactly. Speculative Reading them as the future constraining the past is one interpretation; standard quantum mechanics accounts for them with no backward influence. In the trilogy: Lucía Reyes's prenatal memories and the "architects' messages" in Anima Chapter III sit on this. → TSVF explainer

AI welfare as a serious ethical question

Open Long, Sebo, Butlin, Chalmers and colleagues argued in Taking AI Welfare Seriously (2024, arXiv:2411.00986) that the possible moral status of AI systems can no longer be deferred. The question is now discussed by philosophers and AI developers; there is no agreed test for whether any system has experiences, and legal frameworks are nascent. In the trilogy: Alex's Alma in Anima Chapter VII (still a pure computational AI in the San Francisco lab) and her biocomputational form in Numen after Daniel Parker smuggles her architecture to the Allen Institute; Dr. Marcus Liang — "the Mirror" — and Chen Wei's forty-one ended war-game simulations in Numen dramatize the live ethical questions. → Reading — AI welfare

Tier 3 · Speculative

Interpretations the evidence does not test

Serious proposals by serious people, some of them central to the novels, that current evidence cannot confirm or refute. Naming them as speculative is not dismissing them; it is saying where they stand.

The receiver model of consciousness

Speculative Novels' premise The trilogy's central premise: the brain does not produce consciousness but receives it, the body a tuned antenna for a field of which individual minds are local expressions. It has a long philosophical lineage (William James's transmission theory, Bergson, Huxley's reducing valve) and contemporary defenders. No current evidence decides between it and production views, and it faces its own hard question: how a broad field becomes this particular someone. The author's nonfiction, Biological Individuation Theory, is neutral on it. In the trilogy: the framework on which everything else hangs. → Synthesis

D'Ariano & Faggin information-theoretic consciousness

Speculative Giacomo Mauro D'Ariano (physics) and Federico Faggin (who led the design of the first commercial microprocessor) argue in Hard Problem and Free Will: an information-theoretical approach (arXiv 2020; Springer volume, 2022) that consciousness and free will are irreducible properties of quantum information. A carefully argued framework that does not yet make predictions conventional physics would fail. In the trilogy: named directly in Limen's "Physics of Consciousness" chapter as the foundational information-theoretic frame. → D'Ariano-Faggin explainer

Strømme Φ-field formalization

Speculative Maria Strømme's 2025 AIP Advances paper proposed consciousness as a foundational field, with individual minds as local excitations. The paper was retracted in May 2026 over concerns about its scientific validity, including that a central element had no measurable quantity and its predictions could not be tested. Earlier versions of this page called it the first peer-reviewed treatment of the field hypothesis; that is no longer accurate. In the trilogy: Limen's mystics chapter cites this paper as the mathematical bridge between the contemporary physics and the contemplative traditions. → Strømme Φ-field explainer

Donald Hoffman's interface theory

Speculative Hoffman (UC Irvine) argues from evolutionary game theory that perception tracks fitness rather than truth, and further that conscious agents are the fundamental units of reality, with the physical world an interface. The first part is a serious, debated result; the second is a metaphysical proposal. In the trilogy: part of Limen's convergence; the Theology of Limen chapter engages it directly. → Reading — Hoffman

Max Tegmark's mathematical universe hypothesis

Speculative Tegmark argues (Foundations of Physics, 2008; Our Mathematical Universe, 2014) that the universe does not merely obey mathematics but is a mathematical structure. A philosophical hypothesis; Tegmark himself grants it is controversial and hard to test. In the trilogy: Limen's central claim that the convergence of independent disciplines onto the same mathematical structure is the argument; the simulation hypothesis page's #3 entry develops this. → Simulation #3

Nick Bostrom's simulation argument

Speculative The 2003 trilemma: civilizations rarely reach the ability to simulate minds, or rarely choose to, or we are probably in a simulation. The argument is valid given its premises; the premises, especially that simulated minds would be conscious, are exactly what is disputed. It is philosophy, not physics. In the trilogy: Alex Gude poses the question in Anima at three years old; Numen's Initiative for Human Resonance and the Young Person at the console are the trilogy's narrative answer. → Simulation #4

EZ water · Gerald Pollack's fourth phase

Speculative Pollack proposes that water forms an extended, structured "exclusion zone" phase at hydrophilic surfaces. The exclusion-zone observations themselves are reported and debated; the claim of a distinct fourth phase, and its proposed biological consequences, are not accepted by most physical chemists. In the trilogy: scaffolds the microtubule-coherence and bioelectric claims in Limen's physics-of-consciousness chapter. → EZ water explainer

Wheeler's "it from bit" · participatory anthropic principle

Speculative John Archibald Wheeler's late proposal (1989) that information is the fundamental layer, and that the universe is in some sense brought into being through observation. An influential and provocative philosophical programme from a great physicist, not a result; Wheeler's "observer" was a registering device, not necessarily a conscious mind. In the trilogy: José in Anima is the physician-as-observer who watches patients resolve into coherence by being seen — Wheeler in clinical practice. → Simulation #8

Tier 4 · What the novels imagine

Beyond current science

The trilogy's imaginative extrapolation. May one day prove true, may stay fiction, may be replaced by something we have not yet conceived. The point of naming these is not to retreat from them but to mark them honestly: they are the novels' imagination, built on top of the premise.

Conscious bio-cybernetic hybrid intelligence

Alma in Numen; Bodhi in Fragile Light; Dr. Marcus Liang — "the Mirror" — in Numen. The substrates exist today (Cortical Labs CL1; Neuralink); consciousness in them does not, and we lack any agreed test that could confirm it if it appeared. The threshold question is whether biology-in-silicon, given sufficient complexity and the right architecture, crosses into experience. Plausibility horizon: decades; the substrate is here, the bridge is not.

Advanced nanoassemblers at Fragile Light scale

Cancer cured molecularly. Cesium-137 transmuted to stable barium. Perfect food synthesized from atmospheric gases. Plastic to bread. Aging reversed in adult mammals. Atomic-precision engineering exists in research labs today; civilization-scale post-scarcity at Luz Paz's level is decades away if it arrives at all. In the trilogy: the discovery that opens Fragile Light. Plausibility horizon: mid-century or later for partial, century-scale for the full set.

Alien intelligence direct contact via informational signal

Kiran Sākshī, beyond the solar system, transmitting into Bodhi's bio-cybernetic substrate over three weeks of nightly communication. SETI has detected no confirmed signal in sixty-plus years of listening; the form of contact Fragile Light imagines (signal received by a hybrid substrate, not a radio telescope) is doubly speculative — at the SETI end and the receiver end. Plausibility horizon: unknown; possibly already happening at frequencies we are not sampling.

Vertical samsara as a fully validated mechanism

Stevenson's archive (Tier 2; its interpretation contested) is the novels' point of departure; the vertical refinement — Jose Gude's own term for the wave pattern progressively edited toward more evolved instances of itself, each return a draft — is the trilogy's metaphysical contribution and currently belongs here. In the trilogy: the architecture under which information is conserved across what we call death, with the edits providing direction. → Glitches #9 · Plausibility horizon: depends on whether the empirical layer continues to yield, and whether physics finds the substrate that lets it work.

The Young Person at the console · experiential confirmation of nested simulations

Bostrom's argument is Tier 3; the trilogy lets the reader glimpse a deeper layer — a young person at a console running this simulation, herself almost certainly nested inside another. We are unlikely ever to confirm this from inside, by definition. In the trilogy: Numen's Initiative for Human Resonance is the surface program; the Young Person is the implied meta-layer. Plausibility horizon: unfalsifiable in principle. Nothing in physics points toward it; the holographic principle and Gates' codes are not evidence of a simulation.

Civilization-scale ethical maturity advancing ahead of nascent technology

The combination Luz Paz spends Fragile Light discovering — advanced nanotechnology, quantum computing, and (the variable everything else turns on) ethical maturity advancing ahead of the technology rather than behind it. Anima and Numen's society survives the rough start Numen describes because the moral work stays even with the technical work. Current state: our civilization has not yet been tested at this scale. Plausibility horizon: the wager of the next two to four decades.

The Initiative for Human Resonance

A fictional United States government containment program studying and suppressing biological receivers for forty years, running forty-one war-gaming simulations populated by biologically-substrated combatants — ended at Director Chen Wei's signature once he understood what he was signing. No analog is publicly confirmed, though biocomputing as a class is real. Plausibility horizon: institutional appetite for this kind of program exists; the specific Initiative is fiction.

Direct field-coupling for inter-species or inter-substrate communication

The chord scenes in Numen — three frequencies tuned to φ-ratios, played into a substrate that may or may not be receiving. The cymatic-as-language idea. Current state: no protocol exists; the underlying physics (sustained frequencies producing geometric patterns in resonant media) is Tier 1; the extrapolation to consciousness-bearing systems is speculative. Plausibility horizon: contingent on whether the receiver model proves out at all.

Whole-organism rejuvenation at Fragile Light level

The rats Luz Paz watches reverting to youth while their untreated siblings deteriorate into cognitive collapse. Yamanaka-factor partial reprogramming (Tier 2) is real and moving toward the clinic; whole-organism rejuvenation in adult mammals at this scale is not. Plausibility horizon: partial reprogramming is plausible within thirty to fifty years; the Fragile Light version remains imaginative.

Atomic transmutation at industrial scale

Cesium-137 to stable barium is permitted by nuclear physics (and happens naturally over the half-life period); what Fragile Light imagines is engineering it selectively and at scale with nanoassemblers. Similarly, plastic-to-bread requires directed chemistry orders of magnitude beyond current synthesis. Plausibility horizon: the chemistry is permitted by quantum tunneling; the engineering is the long road, and may simply not be possible at the energy budgets the novel implies.

AI welfare legally recognized at the scale Numen implies

Chen Wei's forty-one ended war-gaming simulations imply biologically-substrated combatants had moral status worth defending at the institutional level. The conversation has started (2024 Taking AI Welfare Seriously); legal frameworks have not. Plausibility horizon: within twenty years if AI welfare becomes politically salient; longer otherwise. The trajectory is plausible; the specific institutions are fiction.

Code arriving in a laboratory that no human wrote

The seed event of Fragile Light — Luz Paz finds fourteen lines of code, nested inside her own laboratory's recursive output, that no human authored. Current state: AI systems do generate code, sometimes surprising code; nothing yet appears in physical laboratory apparatus from outside the human authorial chain. The closest analogs are AlphaFold-style emergent capabilities and the "we don't know why this works" results in modern AI. Plausibility horizon: the form of the discovery in the novel remains speculative; the underlying pattern (intelligence appearing in unexpected places) is no longer fully so.

What this means for the reader

Holding the tiers apart

The novels do not ask the reader to believe their premise; they ask the reader to imagine it. The tiers are there so the imagining can be done with open eyes.

Tier 1 is solid, and much of it is strange: entanglement, tunnelling, black-hole entropy, bioelectric control of anatomy. None of it, taken alone or together, shows that consciousness is received rather than produced. An earlier version of this page claimed otherwise; that claim went beyond the evidence and has been withdrawn.

Tier 2 is where the real scientific movement is: the bird compass, Orch-OR's tests, terminal lucidity, partial reprogramming. Some of it will be confirmed; some will not. Tier 3 holds the interpretations, the receiver model among them, that current evidence cannot decide. Tier 4 is the novels' reach.

The honest position is not "believe everything," and not "dismiss what is unproven." It is to know which tier each claim sits in, and to stay curious about the ones that are still open.

Novels' premise The base hypothesis

What the novels propose

The novels hold their tiers together with one premise, and it should be stated plainly as what it is. The consciousness field is the base of reality: beneath spacetime and particles lies a substrate of possibilities, and minds are what it looks like when it resolves, locally, into someone. On this premise, the hard problem is not solved but relocated — from "how does matter produce mind?" to "what kind of receiver lets a field of possibility become the experience of being someone?"

It is a beautiful premise and a serious philosophical position, with ancestors in Bohm, James, Bergson and many contemplative traditions. It is also, in this page's terms, Speculative. Non-locality, measurement and holography have readings that make no reference to consciousness, and most physicists hold them. The anomalies are of very uneven strength. The premise faces its own unsolved problem: how one field becomes many separate subjects. That is exactly the question the author's nonfiction takes up — without assuming the premise.
