Synopsis

Fragile Light is the novel that arrived between drafts of Numen and would not wait. It is not part of the trilogy. It does not need the trilogy's vocabulary. What it shares with the other books is the question underneath them — what is worth carrying when the structure that asked you to carry it has finally let go — and the conviction that the answer is not abstract.

The book moves quietly. It refuses to give up its weight in summary. The reader who finishes it will know what it is about; the reader who tries to describe it to someone else may find that the description doesn't hold. That is by design.

Themes

What survives

Not memory. Not narrative. The thing that holds across the gap between who you were and who is still going.

Carried weight

The difference between the burdens we chose, the burdens we were handed, and the burdens that turn out — quietly, only after — to have been the practice we were given.

The fragility that is itself the light

The title is not metaphorical in the way the cover suggests. It is functional. The light that survives is fragile because it survived.
